# Coffee Shop Business Case Study | Business Analysis

## Project Overview

This project brings together the findings from my Excel, SQL, Python and Power BI analysis into a business case study for Morning Street Coffee Co.

Using six months of transaction data across three New York City locations, I evaluated where the business should focus its attention across staffing, menu decisions and future growth.

The purpose of this project is to demonstrate my ability to move beyond technical analysis and communicate evidence based recommendations while recognising the limits of the available data.

---

## Business Question

**Given six months of transaction history across three stores, what should the business prioritise first: staffing, menu decisions or expansion, and which recommendations are supported strongly enough by the data to act on?**

---

## Dataset

The case study uses transaction data from **1 January to 30 June 2023** across three New York City coffee shop locations.

Key figures include:

- **149,116 transactions**
- **$698,812.33 total revenue**
- **214,470 units sold**
- **$4.69 average transaction value**
- **80 products across 9 product categories**

---

## Analysis Approach

The case study builds on the previous three portfolio projects:

- **Project 1, Excel:** established the core KPIs and sales patterns.
- **Project 2, SQL and Python:** provided deeper analysis of store performance, product rankings, hourly revenue and pricing consistency.
- **Project 3, Power BI:** presented the findings in an interactive management dashboard.
- **Project 4, Business Case Study:** translates the combined findings into business recommendations and clearly states what the data cannot support.

## Key Findings

### Store Performance

Revenue is distributed evenly across the three stores. Hell’s Kitchen contributed **33.9%**, Astoria **33.2%**, and Lower Manhattan **32.9%** of total revenue.

This suggests that there is no single underperforming store requiring a turnaround plan.

### Morning Demand

The **8–10 AM** period accounts for approximately **36.7% of total revenue**. Revenue per operating hour during this period is approximately **2.1 times** the level observed during the 12–5 PM afternoon period.

This provides a strong reason to review staffing coverage during the morning peak, although the transaction data alone cannot prove that the stores are currently understaffed.

### Revenue Growth

Revenue recorded **three consecutive months of double digit month on month growth from March to May**. June remained positive at **+6.2%**, but additional months of data would be needed before treating the pattern as a long term trend.

### Product Performance

**Sustainably Grown Organic Lg** and **Latte Rg** rank among the top three products at all three stores.

**Civet Cat** is the leading product at Hell’s Kitchen but does not rank among the top three at the other two locations, showing that some merchandising decisions may benefit from store specific analysis.

### Data Quality

**15 of 80 products** were recorded at more than one historical unit price.

This should be validated with the business before conducting detailed pricing, margin or profitability analysis.

---

## Business Recommendations

Based on the evidence, the case study recommends:

- Pilot a shift in available labour coverage towards the **8–10 AM** peak and measure the impact before making a wider staffing change.
- Maintain strong visibility and availability for products that perform consistently across all locations.
- Use store specific product performance when planning local merchandising decisions.
- Validate historical price variation before conducting pricing or margin analysis.
- Continue collecting additional monthly data before making longer term growth assumptions.
- Do not make a fourth location decision from this transaction dataset alone.

---

## What the Data Cannot Answer

The dataset does not include staffing schedules, labour costs, queue times, property costs, foot traffic, customer level information or competitor data.

For this reason, the case study does not claim to determine exact staffing requirements or recommend a fourth store location. Additional operational and market data would be required for those decisions.
